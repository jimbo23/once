# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Wedding guests (ages 20-70+) attending Kiefer's wedding on January 2nd, 2027. They scan a QR code at the venue with their phone — no app download, no account creation. They enter their first name and start shooting. Tech-savvy and non-tech-savvy alike. ~200 guests expected, ~100 active photographers.

## Product Purpose

A digital disposable camera that recreates the anticipation and surprise of film photography for a single wedding. Guests capture candid moments throughout the day; the album "develops" the morning after (January 3rd, 10am) and everyone experiences the reveal together. The couple gets a collective album of perspectives they'd otherwise never see.

## Brand Personality

Nostalgic, bold, fun.

Like finding a roll of undeveloped film in a jacket pocket — warm, surprising, a little imperfect. Not precious or polished. Analog warmth meets digital convenience. The whole experience should feel like holding a disposable camera: tactile, immediate, joyful.

## Anti-references

- Sterile SaaS dashboards
- Minimalist white-space wedding sites (Squarespace template energy)
- Anything that feels like a tech product rather than a physical artifact
- Overly precious or delicate design that's afraid to be bold
- Corporate photo-sharing tools (Google Photos, iCloud Shared Albums)

## Design Principles

1. **Film is the medium** — every interaction references analog photography: rolls, shutters, grain, developing, light leaks. The metaphor isn't decoration — it's the UX.
2. **Capture over compose** — never slow someone down when they're trying to take a photo. The camera UI is fast and gets out of the way. Beauty lives in the output, not the input.
3. **Anticipation is the feature** — the delayed reveal isn't a limitation, it's the whole point. Build tension through the developing state.
4. **Imperfection is warmth** — grain, light leaks, warm shifts. Perfect is cold. The aesthetic embraces what makes film feel human.
5. **One night, one roll** — this isn't a product for repeat use. Every design decision optimizes for a single, unforgettable event.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA minimum (guests span all ages including elderly relatives)
- Camera UI must work one-handed (guests are holding drinks, plates)
- Large touch targets for the shutter button
- Reduced motion alternative for reveal animations
- High contrast text over textured/grain backgrounds
